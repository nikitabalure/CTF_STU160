In this challenge, I acted as an AI detective to find a manipulated book and hidden clues inside its reviews. I was given two datasets: one with book information and one with reviews. First, I computed a unique hash from my student ID (STU160) using SHA256 and used the first eight characters of this hash as a key to search inside the review texts. I then filtered the books to only those with rating_number = 1234 and average_rating = 5.0, and joined these suspicious books with their reviews. The review containing my hash identified both the fake review and the manipulated book.

Next, I derived FLAG1 from the book title by taking the first eight non-space characters of the title and computing their SHA256 hash. FLAG2 was simply the hash itself wrapped in the required format. For FLAG3, I focused on all reviews of the manipulated book and built a simple text classifier. Suspicious reviews were defined as short, very positive five-star reviews with superlative words, while genuine reviews were longer and used more specific, book-related language. Using TF-IDF features with a logistic regression model, I applied SHAP to genuine reviews and extracted the top three words that reduced suspicion the most. These words, combined with my numeric ID, were hashed again to produce FLAG3. This task helped me connect hashing, data filtering, and explainable AI in a single pipeline.
